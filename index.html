§1

Introduction

Small and Medium Scale Manufacturing Enterprises (MSMEs) form the backbone of metal manufacturing in regions such as Punjab, Haryana, and Rajasthan. Surface quality is critical — visible scratches significantly reduce product value and lead to rejection by downstream manufacturers.

Manual inspection is subjective, inconsistent, and prone to fatigue errors. Large-scale Automated Optical Inspection (AOI) systems are prohibitively expensive for MSMEs. This project bridges that gap with a low-cost, explainable, classical CV pipeline running on standard CPUs.

😴

Human Fatigue

Accuracy drops sharply over long shifts — systematic missed detections compound over time.

⚖️

Inconsistent Detection

Subjective judgements vary between inspectors and shifts, making results non-reproducible.

💸

Late-Stage Rejections

Defects found at assembly or shipping cause expensive rework and customer dissatisfaction.

🏭

MSME Constraints

Small workshops cannot afford AOI systems or GPU hardware — the solution must be CPU-only.

§2

Problem Statement

Manual inspection in small-scale metal manufacturing is unreliable. Existing automated systems are expensive and impractical for MSMEs. This project develops a low-cost, classical CV-based binary classifier for surface scratch detection suitable for MSME environments.

IN — Input

Standard RGB Images

Camera images of metal component surfaces. No specialised imaging hardware required.

OUT — Output

Binary Classification

Scratch or No-Defect — JSON response with filename and confidence %.

CON — Constraints

Classical CV Only

No deep learning. CPU-based inference. Works with ~300 labeled samples per class.

TGT — Target Users

MSME Workshops

Metal workshops in Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan producing sheet-metal parts for automotive and agricultural markets.

§3

Literature Review

Earlier research relied heavily on classical techniques including edge detection, texture analysis, and statistical pattern recognition. Methods based on Canny, Gabor filters, LBP, and HOG were commonly used for detecting scratches and cracks on metal surfaces. Gap identified: Limited work targets affordable, explainable, lightweight screening systems specifically for small-scale metal manufacturing.

Canny Edge Detection

Linear discontinuities on surfaces. Scratch patterns appear as elongated edge clusters — the basis of our segmentation step.

GLCM Texture Features

Second-order statistics (contrast, homogeneity) proven effective for surface anomaly characterisation on small datasets.

LBP & HOG

Rotation-invariant texture and gradient descriptors. Identified as future work to address the pipeline's faint-scratch limitation.

SVM Classification

Maximum-margin classifier — well-established for high-confidence binary decisions on compact, well-engineered feature vectors.
